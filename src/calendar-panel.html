Historical keys and limits

The first seven Younger Futhark runes form a repeating weekday row. This lab reports the conventional cycle labels A–G, not fixed weekday names: a year and calendar alignment are required.

The golden-number row uses the sixteen Younger Futhark signs plus Arlaug (17), Tvimadur (18), and Belgthor (19). The l/m order variant is exposed because historical rune rows can differ at positions 14–15. Elder Futhark transliteration is a separate comparison, not a calendar-number system. Ingwaz ᛜ/ᛝ therefore has no value in the 19-symbol calendar key.

Automatic candidates use glyph templates rendered with Noto Sans Runic. Only explicitly enumerated long-branch and short-twig variants are included. Source samples use reviewed pixel boundaries shared across methods, so methods cannot change the character count to improve their coverage. Results do not reuse the Russian letters as rune assignments.

Recorded sensitivity audit · 360 comparisons

Six crops were tested in four whole-line orientations and at three score/lead settings, using five interpretations. This is a bounded shape experiment. Low thresholds can assign numbers to non-runic shapes; full coverage alone is not a decipherment.

Date checks

Modern Gregorian checks for the dates visible in the artwork. These are independent of the image interpretation and cannot turn unmatched symbols into a date.

Controls and reproducibility

Controls verify symbol lookup, rejected lookalikes, retained unknown positions, date arithmetic and the reference-font image recognizer. They do not establish recognition accuracy on ancient carvings or prove that this artwork encodes a calendar.
